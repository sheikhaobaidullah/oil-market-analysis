## Oil market context

The oil price is set by the balance between supply, demand and inventories.
Supply comes from producers worldwide. OPEC+ tries to manage its output,
while US shale producers respond quickly to price changes and act as a
counterweight. Demand follows the global economy: when demand is high,
buyers compete for limited supply and prices rise, and when it falls,
prices drop. Inventories are the buffer between the two. High stocks suggest
supply is running ahead of demand, which tends to push prices down, and low
stocks suggest a tight market, which tends to push prices up. In 2020, COVID
lockdowns caused demand to collapse, stocks filled up, and prices fell
sharply. This project examines how prices, production and inventories moved
before, during and after that shock.
